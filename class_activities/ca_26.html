Class activity: Fishing

Planning a fishing trip

You have 5 days off from work, and you are planning to go on a fishing trip at a nearby state park. Once you pack all your gear, your car and camper van have space for four people total (you and three others). Now you just need to figure out who to bring!

You have two options:

  • You have three college friends who also enjoy fishing, and have been angling for an invitation to join your trip
  • You have three young nieces and nephews who will be on spring break during your trip, and who have also asked to join

You can’t take everyone, so you’ll have to choose between these two options. You would prefer to take your nieces and nephews if possible, but you are worried they will prevent you from doing enough fishing. You decide that you will take your nieces and nephews, if you are confident that the probability that your group catches at least one fish over the five days is at least 80%.

Data

Because you are a statistician, you decide to use data to help make your decision. You have data on 250 groups of park guests who visited the state park. For each group, you have:

  • count: the number of fish caught by the group
  • camper: whether the group brought a camper van (0 = no, 1 = yes)
  • child: the number of children in the group
  • persons: the total number of people in the group
  • LOS: length of stay (in days)

The data can be downloaded into R with the following code:

library(tidyverse)
fish <- read_csv("https://sta214-f25.github.io/homework/fish2.csv")

To model the number of fish caught, we fit a ZIP model:

library(pscl)

m_zip <- zeroinfl(count ~ camper + child + persons,
               offset = log(LOS),
               data = fish)
